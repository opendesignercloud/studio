Compress image

Make images smaller so pages load faster and files fit in emails and uploads. Set the quality, limit the size, and see how much each image shrank. It all happens in your browser, so your images are never uploaded.

    Options

    Output format

    75

    Lower quality makes smaller files. 70 to 80 is a good balance for photos. It does not change PNG files that keep their format.

    Smaller images are never enlarged.

    Background

    JPG cannot be transparent, so transparent areas get this colour.

    How it works

    1. Drop in one image or a whole batch.
    2. Leave the format on Keep, or switch to JPG, WebP or AVIF for smaller files. Adjust the quality and, if you want, the maximum size. Results appear straight away and update when you change an option.
    3. Check the saving on each image, then download them one at a time, or all at once as a ZIP.

    How much smaller will my images get?

    It depends on the image. Large camera photos often shrink by more than half at quality 75 with no visible difference at normal viewing size. Screenshots and logos saved as PNG shrink little, because PNG is lossless. Switching those to WebP usually saves much more. Limiting the longest side to 1920 pixels is another big saving for photos that will only be shown on a screen.

    Will it ever make a file bigger?

    Not when you keep the format. If recompressing an image would not make it smaller, you get your original back and it is marked as already well compressed. If you switch format, the list shows the exact change, even when a file grows.

    Are my images uploaded?

    No. Your images are compressed by your own browser and never leave your device. To understand how the tools are used, we keep cookie-free page-view counts and simple usage counts (which tool ran and whether it worked). We never see your images, file names or personal details.

    What happens to photo details and colour profiles?

    Compressing redraws the image, so camera details and location data (EXIF) are removed, which also saves a little space. The colour profile becomes standard sRGB. Files that are returned untouched keep their original details.

    What are the limits?

    Up to 40 images at a time, each up to 60 MB. Images larger than 8192 pixels on the longest side are reduced to fit. Animated GIFs are compressed from their first frame, and SVG files are drawn at 2048 pixels. iPhone HEIC photos need to be exported as JPG or PNG first.
